Trench Arcane - Comprehensive Rules Outline

Version 1.5 - Order of Operations Update

100. Game Zones

Instead of a single battlefield, Trench Arcane utilizes a distinct spatial layout that requires physical advancement and retreat.

101. Player 1 Base: The starting zone for Player 1. It contains the player's 6 Resource cards, Buildings, the Command Center, and newly deployed Units.

102. The Field: The neutral, central zone connecting the two bases.

103. Player 2 Base: The starting zone for Player 2.

104. Zone Capacity: There is no maximum limit to the number of Units, Buildings, or entities a player may have in any given zone.

105. Zone Occupation & Transience: It is expected that units of opposing sides will inhabit the same zone, as this is the first gate to pass when deciding if a target is valid. There is no default pinning or interception; units may move into or past zones occupied by enemy units. A unit that is transient because it is moving two zones (e.g., via the Swift keyword) is not considered present in the bypassed zone at the time of targeting.

200. The Resource System

201. Persistent Resources: A player begins the game with exactly 6 Resource cards deployed in their Base. They may consist of any combination of Biomass, Metal, and Aether.

202. Resource Generation & Capacity: During the Resource Generation phase, each Resource card automatically produces 1 resource of its type. A single Resource card has a maximum capacity of 3 banked resources. If a card is at maximum capacity, it cannot generate more until spent.

203. Ephemeral Resources: Temporary resources generated by card effects or mechanics (such as Pillage). If a cost requires a specific resource type, players must spend Ephemeral resources of that type before spending banked resources. Ephemeral resources cannot be banked and vanish at the end of the turn.

204. Resource Tracking: Players use tokens or dice to accurately track the quantity of banked resources currently held on a Resource card.

300. Card Types

301. Resource: The foundation of a player's economy and real estate. Resources can be targeted by attacks, but only if no Building is occupying them.

302. Building: Persistent structures placed directly onto a Resource card. Only one building can occupy a Resource card at a time. Buildings possess Health and Armor. A Building cannot move, but it may possess an Attack stat to participate in base defense.

302a. Overbuilding: A player may build over an existing structure on their Resource. The original Building is destroyed and replaced.

302b. Command Center (Subtype): A unique proxy for the player, possessing Health, Armor, and potentially unique abilities or an Attack stat. A player chooses exactly one unique Command Center to anchor their deck's theme and strategy. It cannot be targeted by "destroy" effects. If a player's Command Center is destroyed, state-based actions dictate that they lose the game immediately.

303. Unit: Entities deployed strictly into a player's Base zone. Units are responsible for movement and combat. Damage on units persists across turns.

304. Command: Spells that can be played at any time a player has priority. A Command is placed on the Stack and remains pending until it resolves or is countered. After the effect resolves, the Command card is placed into the discard pile.

400. Turn Structure

401. Turn Start: The beginning of the turn is divided into two distinct steps.

401a. Upkeep: Initial triggers, resets, and modifiers are processed.

401b. Draw Step: The turn player draws exactly 2 cards from their deck.

402. Resource Generation: All non-capped Resource cards bank 1 resource of their respective type.

403. Pre-Volley Deployment: The turn player may spend resources to deploy Units into their Base, construct Buildings, or play Commands.

404. Movement: Units may move to an adjacent zone. Moving toward the opponent's Base is considered Advancing; moving toward the player's own Base is considered Retreating.

405. Volley: The turn player declares attacks.

406. Post-Volley Deployment: The turn player receives a second deployment window. They may spend any remaining banked resources, as well as any Ephemeral resources generated during the Volley phase, to deploy Units, construct Buildings, or play Commands.

407. End: Cleanup step. Damage markings may clear (depending on specific card effects), unspent Ephemeral resources cease to exist, and players discard down to their maximum hand size.

500. Combat & Interactions

501. Pillage (Resource Theft): A combat interaction triggered when an unprotected Resource card is successfully attacked.

501a. Pillage Resolution: When an exposed Resource takes damage from a unit, the attacker removes a banked resource from it.

501b. Pillage Reward: The attacker gains this as an Ephemeral resource for their current turn. The type of Ephemeral resource gained matches the type produced by the pillaged Resource card.

501c. Ephemeral Limits: Ephemeral resources cannot be banked and vanish at the end of the turn.

600. Unit Attributes

Units (and Buildings, where applicable) possess three core statistics that govern their effectiveness in combat.

601. Attack: The amount of damage the entity deals during an Engagement.

602. Health: The amount of damage the entity can sustain. Because damage is persistent across turns, an entity is destroyed and placed in the discard pile the moment its accumulated damage equals or exceeds its Health.

603. Armor (Damage Threshold & Sundering): Armor acts as a minimum defense threshold that must be sundered before the target can take damage to its Health. Armor can be sundered in one of two ways during an Engagement:

603a. Penetration (Single Unit): A single attacking unit's Attack value equals or exceeds the target's Armor value.

603b. Overwhelm (Multiple Units): The total number of individual units engaging the target strictly exceeds the target's Armor value.

603c. Deflection: If the armor is not sundered by either penetration or overwhelming numbers, all incoming attacks are deflected, and exactly 0 damage is dealt.

700. Movement Constraints & Action Economy

701. Deployment Sickness: A Unit cannot undergo a Move action during the Movement phase of the turn it was deployed.

702. Immediate Defense: Deployment sickness only restricts Movement. A newly deployed unit may still declare attacks during the Volley phase, provided there are valid enemy targets within range.

703. Action Independence: A unit that performs a normal move during the Movement phase may also attack during the Volley phase by default. Taking a move action does not forfeit the ability to attack.

800. Combat Resolution (The Volley Phase Expanded)

The Volley phase follows a strict Order of Operations. During this phase, the turn player acts as the attacker. Engagements are declared and resolved one by one, following these sequential steps:

801. Step 1: Declaring Engagements (Zone Logic): The attacking player chooses one or more active Units they control and declares a single valid enemy Unit, Building, or exposed Resource as the target.

801a. Zone Restriction: An attacker can only target enemy entities that are currently occupying the exact same zone as the attacking unit(s), unless modified by a keyword (e.g., Artillery).

801b. The Stack: This declared Engagement is placed on the Stack.

802. Step 2: The Combat Response Window: Once the Engagement is declared on the Stack, a priority window opens. Players may play Command cards or activate abilities in response to the declaration.

803. Step 3: Damage Resolution & Reciprocal Exchange: Once both players pass priority in Step 2, the Engagement resolves. Crucially, there is no opportunity for either player to respond between Damage Resolution and the Reciprocal Damage Exchange. This is a single, locked step processed simultaneously:

803a. Armor Calculation: The game checks if the defender's Armor is Sundered. It is sundered via Penetration (a single attacker's Attack value $\ge$ the Armor) OR via Overwhelm (the total number of attacking units is strictly $>$ the Armor). If neither is true, 0 damage is dealt.

803b. Simultaneous Strike: If the Armor is sundered, the attackers deal their combined full Attack value to the defender's Health. At the exact same time, if the defender possesses an Attack stat, it deals its full Attack value back to the attacker(s) as reciprocal damage.

804. Step 4: Distributed Retaliation (Group Engagements): If multiple units ganged up on a single target, the defending unit still reciprocates its full Attack value. The attacking player chooses how this incoming reciprocating damage is distributed among their attacking units.

805. Step 5: Combat Cleanup: State-based actions are checked. Any unit whose total accumulated damage equals or exceeds its Health is destroyed and placed in the discard pile. Pillage rewards (if an exposed Resource was successfully damaged) are generated.

806. Combat Example

The Board State: Player A has three Charnel Fodder (1 Attack / 0 Armor / 1 Health) in The Field. Player B has one Diesel Dreadnought (3 Attack / 1 Armor / 5 Health) in The Field.

Step 1 (Declaration): It is Player A's Volley Phase. Player A declares a Group Engagement: all three of their Charnel Fodder target the Diesel Dreadnought. Because they are in the same zone, the targeting is valid.

Step 2 (Response Window): The priority window opens. Neither player plays a Command card. The engagement resolves.

Step 3 (Armor Check & Exchange): The Diesel Dreadnought has 1 Armor. None of the Charnel Fodder possess an Attack $\ge$ 1, so they cannot sunder it via Penetration. However, there are 3 attackers. Because 3 attackers $>$ 1 Armor, the armor is sundered via Overwhelm. The Charnel Fodder deal a combined 3 damage to the Diesel Dreadnought's Health. Simultaneously, the Diesel Dreadnought reciprocates with its 3 Attack. Neither player may play a Command card during this exchange.

Step 4 (Distribution): Player A must distribute the Dreadnought's 3 reciprocating damage among their attackers. They assign 1 damage to each of the three Charnel Fodder.

Step 5 (Cleanup): The Diesel Dreadnought survives with 3 damage marked on it (5 Health - 3 Damage = 2 Health remaining). All three Charnel Fodder take lethal damage and are destroyed.

900. Deck Construction, Setup & Hand Management

901. Setup & The Command Center: The 6 Resource cards and the 1 Command Center card are chosen prior to the game and placed on the board. They are independent of, and not included in, a player's constructed deck.

902. Deck Size: A constructed deck must contain a minimum of 40 cards and a maximum of 80 cards.

903. Card Copies: A player's deck may contain a maximum of 3 copies of any card with the exact same name.

904. Starting Hand & Mulligan: At the beginning of the game, each player draws an initial hand of 8 cards. A player may choose to mulligan exactly once for free. To do so, they shuffle their initial hand back into their deck and draw a completely new hand of 8 cards.

905. Maximum Hand Size: A player's maximum hand size is 8 cards. If a player has more than 8 cards in their hand during the End step (Rule 407), they must discard cards until they reach the maximum hand size.

906. Deck Depletion (Supply Line Attrition): If a player is required to draw a card during their Draw Step but has 0 cards remaining in their deck, they do not draw. Instead, their Command Center immediately takes damage equal to the total number of turns their deck has been empty (e.g., 1 damage on the first empty turn, 2 damage on the second, 3 on the third). This represents the escalating collapse of their supply lines.

907. Card Costs: Cards will strictly specify what types of resources are required to play them (e.g., Biomass, Metal, or Aether). There is no "general" or generic resource cost used in the game's design.

908. Resource Mixing: Players are not restricted to specific factions. A constructed deck may contain any mix of Biomass, Metal, and Aether cards, provided the player utilizes the appropriate Resource cards in their 6-card setup to pay their costs.

1000. Glossary of Keywords

1001. Piercing: Any unit this entity targets has its Armor reduced to 0 for the duration of the engagement.

1002. Artillery: This unit may only target entities in an adjacent zone. It cannot declare an attack during a turn in which it moved. Additionally, if this unit attacks, it incurs a cooldown and cannot attack during its controller's next turn.

1003. Swift: During the Movement phase, this unit may move up to two zones instead of the standard one. If it chooses to do so, it receives a temporary -1 damage modifier to its Attack value until the start of its controller's next turn.

1004. Suppressive Fire: Prevents enemy units from advancing past the zone currently occupied by this unit.

1005. Sapper: During an engagement with a target possessing the Artillery keyword, this unit's Attack value is doubled. (Note: This doubling occurs before determining if the target's Armor is sundered).

1006. Demolitionist: During an engagement with a target possessing the Building card type, this unit's Attack value is doubled. (Note: This doubling occurs before determining if the target's Armor is sundered).

1100. Priority & The Stack

Trench Arcane utilizes a Last-In, First-Out (LIFO) resolution system to handle interactions between players.

1101. Priority: A player must have "priority" in order to play a Command card or take an action. The active (turn) player receives priority first at the beginning of each phase and step.

1102. The Stack: When a player plays a Command card or declares an Engagement, it does not resolve immediately. Instead, it is placed on "The Stack."

1103. Passing Priority: Whenever a player adds an action to the Stack, they must pass priority to their opponent. The opponent may then respond by playing their own Command card, placing it on top of the Stack.

1104. Resolution (LIFO): Once both players pass priority in succession without taking an action, the topmost item on the Stack resolves. After it resolves, the active player regains priority, and players have another opportunity to act before the next item down on the Stack resolves.
